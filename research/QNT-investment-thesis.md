# Quant (QNT) — Sub-Quadratic Investment Research
**Research date:** 20 November 2026  
**Asset:** Quant Network (QNT)  
**Classification:** Institutional blockchain interoperability / programmable-money infrastructure  
**Committee posture:** Positive strategic thesis; valuation and token-value-capture work required before position sizing.

---

## Executive investment view

Quant is positioned in a part of the digital-asset market that matters to institutional adoption: interoperability between regulated financial institutions, existing payment systems, and multiple public or private ledgers. The investment case is not that one blockchain wins. It is that banks, payment networks, asset managers and corporates will operate across several ledgers and need secure orchestration, settlement and compliance between them.

Quant's product stack addresses that problem through Overledger and, more recently, the Fusion Rollup. The most important development is the move from an interoperability proposition into an institutional execution environment: Quant announced Fusion Rollup mainnet availability in 2026, initially connecting 74 networks. In September 2026, The Clearing House selected Quant technology for its On-Chain Money Initiative, intended to support clearing and settlement of tokenised deposits and connect with existing US payment rails, including RTP and CHIPS.

**Our central thesis:** as money and financial assets become programmable and tokenised, the infrastructure that connects fragmented ledgers to regulated financial markets can become strategically important. Quant is a credible company-level contender in that infrastructure layer. QNT is the associated digital asset, but its investment case must be underwritten separately from the quality of the company and its partnerships.

## 1. The market structure: why interoperability matters

Financial markets were built on systems designed for different eras, institutions and jurisdictions. Cross-border payments, securities settlement, liquidity management and reconciliation still involve multiple intermediaries, operating windows, messaging standards and account structures.

The transition is not simply from “old rails” to “blockchain rails”. The more realistic institutional outcome is a hybrid financial system:
- Commercial-bank deposits represented as tokenised liabilities.
- Regulated stablecoins used for payment and settlement.
- Securities and funds issued or represented on distributed ledgers.
- Existing payment networks retained for reach, resilience, compliance and final settlement.
- Interoperability layers coordinating activity across these environments.

The strategic bottleneck is therefore connectivity and execution across systems, not merely transaction throughput on one chain.

Swift's own programme confirms that the incumbent network is adapting rather than standing still. In July 2026, Swift said 17 banks across six continents were preparing live tokenised-deposit payment pilots on its blockchain-based ledger. This validates the direction of travel, while also establishing Swift as a formidable competitor and potential ecosystem participant.

## 2. Quant's proposition

### Overledger

Overledger is Quant's enterprise interoperability technology. It is designed to connect existing systems and distributed ledgers through a common software interface, reducing the need for institutions to build and maintain bespoke integrations for every network.

The value proposition is practical: institutions can connect systems, coordinate workflows and interact with multiple ledgers without making a single blockchain the centre of their entire architecture.

### Fusion Rollup

Quant announced that Fusion Rollup went live on mainnet in 2026, initially connecting 74 blockchain networks. Quant describes it as a multi-ledger execution environment for institutions, with cross-network asset movement, transaction orchestration and compliance controls.

This matters because the product ambition moves beyond translating messages between networks. It seeks to coordinate activity and liquidity across them. If institutions adopt it for production workflows, Quant could occupy a more embedded position in financial-market infrastructure.

**Investment significance:** the strongest evidence to monitor is not the number of networks listed as connected. It is the number of live institutional workflows, recurring transactions, assets settled, paying customers and fees generated.

## 3. Institutional evidence and strategic relationships

### The Clearing House — September 2026

The Clearing House announced that it selected Quant to provide interoperability, orchestration and transaction-management technology for its On-Chain Money Initiative. The initiative is intended to support clearing and settlement of tokenised deposit transactions and connect to existing payment infrastructure, including RTP and CHIPS. The Clearing House said the network is expected to become available to participating institutions in the first half of 2027.

This is a significant institutional validation point. The Clearing House operates major US payment systems and says its networks clear and settle more than $2 trillion each day. The selection is evidence of a role in a serious banking initiative; it is not yet proof of production-scale adoption or QNT token demand.

### Project Rosalind — BIS and Bank of England

Quant announced that it participated in Project Rosalind as part of the vendor team. The project was led by the Bank for International Settlements Innovation Hub's London Centre with the Bank of England. It explored how APIs could support a retail CBDC system and how central-bank infrastructure could interact with private-sector service providers.

The BIS describes Project Rosalind as a concluded experiment, not a commercial deployment or an endorsement of QNT. Its relevance is that Quant gained experience in a central-bank-led exploration of API-based CBDC infrastructure and public-private interoperability.

### Japan — Dentsu Soken

In January 2026, Quant announced a partnership with Dentsu Soken to support Japanese financial institutions exploring tokenised deposits, institutional stablecoins and programmable settlement infrastructure. Dentsu Soken brings experience integrating mission-critical financial and payment systems.

The strategic relevance is geographic and operational: institutional digital-money infrastructure is not a single-market opportunity.

### Partner and ecosystem references

Quant's published partner material includes UST, LACChain, SIA/Nexi and Oracle-related ecosystem engagement. Each relationship should be assessed by its precise contractual status, scope, production deployment and revenue contribution. A partner logo or announcement is not equivalent to a paying production customer.

## 4. Leadership

Quant was founded by Gilbert Verdian, who serves as founder and CEO. Quant's published biography describes more than 20 years of cybersecurity experience and senior technology/security roles across public and private-sector organisations, including Mastercard/Vocalink, HSBC, BP, EY and government departments.

Verdian also established the ISO/TC 307 blockchain standards initiative in 2015 and is identified by Quant as convenor of its interoperability working group. This background is strategically relevant: institutional interoperability requires standards, security, governance and integration expertise as much as it requires blockchain engineering.

## 5. QNT: token function and value capture

QNT is not equity in Quant Network Ltd. It does not represent company shares, dividends, voting rights or a claim on company revenue.

Published product and disclosure material identifies several token functions:
1. Overledger licence and platform access payments, where applicable.
2. Fusion Rollup gas and execution fees.
3. QNT staking by trusted-node operators participating in the Fusion framework.

Quant's product documentation and third-party disclosures indicate that platform fees may also be paid in fiat currency or through QNT subscription arrangements. This distinction is material. Adoption of Quant software does not automatically translate one-for-one into open-market QNT purchases.

The token thesis strengthens if:
- Production customers must acquire or lock QNT to access meaningful services.
- Fusion activity creates recurring QNT-denominated gas demand.
- Trusted-node participation requires meaningful QNT stake.
- A growing share of platform economics is structurally routed through QNT.
- Circulating supply available to the market tightens while usage expands.

The thesis weakens if customers can predominantly pay in fiat without creating durable QNT demand, or if network usage grows without meaningful token velocity, lock-up or fee capture.

## 6. Supply architecture

Quant's original token supply is widely reported as approximately 14.612 million QNT, following the 2018 burn of unsold tokens. Third-party disclosures cite approximately 14.544 million circulating at the time of their July 2026 filing, with a small residual balance held by the company.

The fixed and relatively small supply is a potentially favourable structural feature if real token demand expands. Scarcity alone, however, is not an investment thesis. The key variable is the relationship between token-required activity and the amount of QNT available to meet that activity.

For ongoing monitoring, use on-chain contract data and current disclosures rather than stale aggregator figures. Record the observation date whenever supply metrics are quoted.

## 7. Competitive landscape

Quant is not operating in an empty market.

- **Swift:** has announced its own blockchain-based shared ledger and interoperability work with global banks. Its distribution, standards and institutional relationships make it a major incumbent competitor.
- **Hyperledger / enterprise DLT stacks:** provide institutions with permissioned infrastructure and integration options.
- **Chainlink and other interoperability providers:** compete in cross-chain messaging, data and institutional connectivity.
- **Bank-built and consortium networks:** may choose proprietary or standards-based architectures rather than an external interoperability layer.

Quant's differentiation is its focus on orchestration across multiple ledgers and its positioning around regulated institutions. The competitive test is whether institutions select Quant for live, recurring workflows and whether those deployments create durable economics for QNT.

## 8. Regulatory and policy backdrop

The US GENIUS Act was signed into law on 18 July 2025. It establishes a federal framework for permitted payment stablecoin issuers, including reserve and supervisory requirements. This is important to the wider thesis because regulated payment stablecoins can become part of institutional settlement infrastructure.

The Act does not specifically endorse Quant or QNT. Its relevance is indirect: it helps define the regulatory perimeter for one category of tokenised money that interoperability infrastructure may need to support.

## 9. Investment thesis — why Sub-Quadratic is researching QNT

The positive case rests on five pillars:

1. **A structural market need:** financial institutions are moving toward tokenised deposits, stablecoins and digital securities, creating demand for connectivity between fragmented systems.
2. **An infrastructure position:** Quant is building middleware and execution technology intended to connect regulated institutions to multiple ledgers.
3. **Institutional validation:** Project Rosalind, the Dentsu Soken partnership and The Clearing House selection provide evidence of engagement in serious institutional programmes.
4. **Product progression:** Fusion Rollup's mainnet launch represents a step from interoperability messaging toward multi-ledger execution and asset coordination.
5. **Constrained token supply with expanding potential utility:** QNT has a relatively small fixed supply and documented product functions. If those functions become essential to production usage, token demand could become more closely linked to network activity.

**Our conviction is in the infrastructure direction first, and in QNT token value capture second.** We want evidence that the token is economically necessary to the network's growth, not merely associated with a successful software company.

## 11. What would change our view

We would increase conviction if Quant demonstrates repeatable production deployments, meaningful transaction throughput, expanding institutional customer use and measurable QNT-denominated demand.

We would reduce conviction if institutional announcements fail to progress into production, if customers can avoid QNT for nearly all meaningful activity, if Fusion usage remains immaterial, or if competitors capture the same workflows with stronger distribution and economics.

## 12. Conclusion

The financial system is not going to become one blockchain. It is becoming a network of banks, payment systems, tokenised deposits, stablecoins, securities ledgers and regulated digital-asset venues. Those systems need to communicate, coordinate and settle with one another.

Quant is positioning itself in that connective tissue. Its institutional relationships, Overledger platform and Fusion Rollup make it a serious infrastructure name to investigate. The Clearing House selection is an especially important proof point to follow through to production in 2027.

For Sub-Quadratic, QNT merits a place on the institutional digital-asset research list because it offers exposure to the infrastructure layer of financial modernisation rather than a single consumer application or speculative blockchain narrative.

**The closing question is not whether the world will digitise money. It is which infrastructure providers become indispensable when it does — and whether QNT captures a material share of that value.**

## 13. Market-capitalisation, supply and price framework
**Market snapshot:** 20 November 2026 (USD; live aggregator snapshots, not an execution quote).

CoinGecko reported QNT at approximately **$145.21**, circulating supply **14.556 million**, market capitalisation **$2.114 billion**, and fully diluted valuation **$2.122 billion**. The reported maximum supply is **14.612 million**. On that dataset, nearly all reported maximum supply is already circulating, so the conventional circulating-market-cap versus FDV gap is small.

There is a material data-quality issue: CoinMarketCap displayed a materially lower circulating-supply figure (approximately 12.07 million) and a different total/max-supply figure in its 20 November snapshot. CoinDesk and CoinGecko showed figures closer to 14.6–14.8 million. Sub-Quadratic will not silently blend these datasets. For this memorandum, the CoinGecko snapshot is the working reference, with the discrepancy flagged for primary-contract and issuer reconciliation before any trade-size decision.

### Working valuation sensitivity

Using 14.556 million QNT as the working circulating-supply denominator:

| Implied market capitalisation | Implied QNT price |
|---:|---:|
| $1.0bn | ~$68.70 |
| $2.5bn | ~$171.75 |
| $5.0bn | ~$343.50 |
| $10.0bn | ~$687.00 |
| $20.0bn | ~$1,374.00 |

These are arithmetic sensitivities, not forecasts, price targets or assigned probabilities. The model assumes the circulating supply remains constant. Actual price formation also depends on available float, liquidity, exchange depth, holder behaviour, derivatives positioning and marginal demand. Market capitalisation is price multiplied by supply; it is not the amount of cash that must enter the asset.

### What the sensitivity tells the committee

At the working snapshot, QNT is a roughly $2.1bn asset, not a low-cap token. A move from approximately $2.1bn to $5bn market capitalisation would imply roughly 2.4 times the price under a constant-supply assumption. A move to $10bn would imply roughly 4.7 times. The investment question is whether institutional adoption, actual token utility and market access can support those valuations relative to competing infrastructure assets.

### Low-cap valuation discipline — applied across the Sub-Quadratic universe

Market capitalisation is a first-order input to price analysis, not a standalone buy signal. For every smaller-cap asset, the memorandum must separately examine:

- **Circulating market cap:** current price multiplied by independently checked circulating supply.
- **FDV:** current price multiplied by total or maximum supply, with the exact denominator stated.
- **Unlocks and emissions:** scheduled vesting, treasury distributions, foundation allocations and future issuance.
- **Effective float:** tokens plausibly available to trade, distinguishing exchange balances, long-term holdings, locked tokens and strategic reserves where verifiable.
- **Liquidity:** spot volume quality, order-book depth, venue concentration, spreads and slippage for the proposed position size.
- **Holder concentration:** top-wallet concentration after identifying contracts, exchanges, custodians, bridges and treasury wallets.
- **Valuation comparables:** market caps of genuinely comparable networks, adjusted for product maturity, adoption, revenue/fees and token economics.
- **Scenario math:** implied token prices at several market-cap levels, plus dilution and float sensitivity.

A smaller market cap can make a given percentage re-rating arithmetically possible with a lower valuation base. It does not guarantee that a modest amount of buying will produce a durable re-rating: thin liquidity can magnify both upward and downward moves, while unlocks and concentrated ownership can overwhelm demand.

### Formula used

`Implied token price = target market capitalisation ÷ assumed circulating supply`

`Market capitalisation = token price × circulating supply`

All scenario tables must state the supply denominator and snapshot date. Never compare two tokens' unit prices without comparing their market capitalisations and supplies.

## 15. Supply reconciliation and revised valuation work — 20 November 2026

### Supply conclusion

For valuation work, use the **14,612,493.08 QNT fixed total supply** as the working maximum supply, not a rounded 27 million figure and not the 45.467 million theoretical constant sometimes displayed by the token contract interface.

The difference between total and circulating supply is therefore approximately **68,317 QNT**, or about **0.47%** of total supply, on that dated record. The filing says the residual company-held balance is unlocked; treat that as a potential supply overhang, not as proof that it will be sold.

CoinGecko's 20 November 2026 snapshot reports 14.556 million circulating and 14.612 million total. The difference between CoinGecko and the dated Etherscan/Blockworks circulating figure is small in percentage terms, but the committee should preserve the exact source and date rather than imply false precision. ### What the low supply does — and does not — tell us

The low fixed supply creates high unit-price sensitivity to changes in the market value assigned to the network. At roughly 14.54 million circulating tokens, each additional $1 billion of market capitalisation corresponds to approximately **$68.76 per QNT**. That is the arithmetic consequence of the denominator.

It does **not** mean that $1 billion of new cash must enter for market capitalisation to rise by $1 billion. Market cap is the marginal traded price multiplied by supply. Nor does low supply by itself establish undervaluation: the market must still justify the total valuation through adoption, token utility, liquidity and investor demand.

### Token demand: the central underwriting issue

The token has several documented roles, but they must be separated carefully:

- Quant's published licence announcement introduced a £100 annual Overledger production licence payable in QNT.
- Quant's current FAQ says platform subscriptions can be paid in USD or by subscribing with QNT. This is important: enterprise software adoption does not automatically force every customer to buy QNT on the open market.
- Quant's terms define QNT as a utility token usable for Quant products and services, including staking. The terms also state that customers may pay platform fees in fiat or QNT depending on the order arrangement.
- Quant's Fusion materials describe QNT as the network's native token and staking mechanism for trusted nodes. These are company descriptions; the committee must verify live network rules, minimum stake, reward source, fee denomination and actual on-chain activity before assigning material value to them.

The investment case therefore depends on whether production usage creates recurring, unavoidable or economically meaningful QNT demand, lock-up or staking. A token that can be bypassed for most customer payments may capture less value than the underlying software company.

### Diligence priorities now open

1. Reconcile total supply and circulating supply against the verified ERC-20 contract, burn transaction and current holder/wallet classification.
2. Identify company, founder, advisor, exchange, custody, bridge and smart-contract wallets before interpreting top-holder concentration.
3. Quantify the amount of QNT held on centralised exchanges and the trend over time, while avoiding the assumption that exchange balances equal available sell-side float.
4. Verify Fusion's live token mechanics: QNT gas denomination, fee routing, staking thresholds, rewards, slashing (if any), node count and active stake.
5. Establish whether customer fees paid in fiat are converted into QNT, retained as fiat, or otherwise create no open-market token demand.
6. Track The Clearing House initiative from selection to pilot, production availability, named participating institutions, transaction volumes and actual Quant/QNT economics.
7. Compare QNT's valuation against infrastructure peers using adoption and token value capture, not unit price or token scarcity alone.

### Updated committee assessment

The fixed supply is a genuine positive structural characteristic. It makes the token's unit price highly sensitive to changes in market capitalisation, and there is little conventional dilution from new issuance on the cited supply record. However, QNT is already a roughly $2.1 billion asset at the current snapshot. The key question is not whether a scarce token can print a high unit price; it is whether Quant can establish a sufficiently large, defensible economic role and whether that role creates durable demand for QNT itself.

**Current research stance: positive watch / valuation diligence in progress. No final entry decision until token value capture, liquidity, wallet concentration and institutional production evidence are independently reconciled.**

## 16. Phase II — token mechanics, demand quality and market structure
**Research update:** 20 November 2026

### A. Supply: current working view

The strongest supply evidence currently available is the Quant token-transparency filing hosted by Blockworks, which cites the post-burn supply record and Etherscan circulating supply:

- Total supply: **14,612,493 QNT**.
- Circulating supply: **14,544,176 QNT** (dated filing figure).
- Difference: approximately **68,317 QNT**, or **0.47%** of total supply.
- Supply regime: fixed; the filing says no additional QNT has been created after the token generation event.
- The 45,467,000 number shown by Etherscan as a contract maximum is a hard-coded theoretical pre-burn figure, not the post-burn number of tokens actually created.

The 68,317 QNT residual is described as company-held and unlocked in the filing. It is small relative to total supply but should remain on the watchlist as a potential source of sell-side supply. Wallet balances and classifications can change; refresh the data before any transaction.

**Source hierarchy:** issuer's original burn explanation and verified on-chain transfer/burn records should take precedence over aggregator supply estimates. The Blockworks filing is a useful consolidated disclosure, but it is not itself the blockchain.

### B. Demand quality: what the customer actually has to do

Quant's FAQ currently says a customer can pay its Overledger platform fee in USD or subscribe with QNT. This is decisive for underwriting: an Overledger customer is not necessarily forced to purchase QNT in the open market.

Quant's historic Overledger licence announcement described a £100 annual production licence payable in QNT. That is evidence of a QNT-denominated access mechanism, but the current FAQ shows that payment choice exists. The old £100 licence should not be extrapolated into current enterprise pricing or aggregate token demand without customer counts and contract terms.

**Committee implication:** the licence mechanism is real utility, but its direct demand impact cannot yet be quantified. It is not sufficient, by itself, to justify a multi-billion-dollar token valuation.

### C. Fusion Rollup: potential step-change, still requires measurement

Quant's mainnet announcement says Fusion launched connected to 74 blockchain networks, with further networks available through its Trusted Node Program. Quant describes Fusion as a multi-ledger execution environment for cross-network asset movements, settlement and messaging.

The company announcement is meaningful evidence that a product has launched. It is not independent evidence of:
- production transaction volume or value settled;
- the number of active institutional customers;
- the amount of QNT held or staked by node operators;
- the amount of QNT consumed as gas or fees;
- fee routing, burning or distribution;
- recurring open-market purchases caused by usage.

These items require current technical documentation, on-chain contract/event analysis and customer deployment evidence. Until those data are collected, do not assign a quantified token-demand premium to the 74-network headline.

### D. Token value capture: provisional scorecard

| Mechanism | What is evidenced | What remains unproven for valuation |
|---|---|---|
| Overledger access/licensing | QNT payment option; historical QNT licence mechanism | Current QNT-paid customer share, volumes, purchase/lock-up behaviour |
| Fiat subscription | Quant FAQ explicitly permits USD payment | Whether fiat revenue is converted into QNT or creates any secondary demand |
| Fusion execution/gas | Quant positions Fusion as a multi-ledger execution environment | Exact live fee denomination, fee levels, payment routing and QNT consumption |
| Trusted-node staking | Quant describes a Trusted Node Program and staking framework | Current active stake, minimums, lock duration, rewards, slashing and decentralisation |
| Supply constraint | Fixed total supply in issuer disclosure; ~14.54m circulating | Current beneficial ownership, exchange float and genuine sellable float |
| Governance/equity rights | QNT is a utility token, not company equity | No equity claim or token-holder governance right should be priced in |

**Provisional conclusion:** QNT has defined utility and a credible route to additional utility through Fusion, but public evidence reviewed in this phase does not yet quantify the conversion of product adoption into persistent net token demand. That is the most important unresolved part of the investment case.

### G. What would make the present valuation attractive?

The current market capitalisation is already approximately $2.1bn. A positive entry thesis requires evidence that one or more of the following can become durable:

1. The Clearing House initiative reaches production and Quant's role is material, repeatable and economically valuable.
2. Additional banks, payment networks, asset managers or market infrastructures deploy Quant in live workflows.
3. Fusion creates measurable QNT-denominated gas or execution demand, not just software usage.
4. Trusted-node staking locks a meaningful proportion of circulating QNT for sustained periods, with transparent reward economics.
5. Enterprise subscription choices and contract structures create recurring QNT acquisition or lock-up rather than allowing fiat payment to bypass token demand entirely.
6. Exchange float tightens while demand rises, verified through wallet-level data rather than assumed from low total supply.

### H. Falsification and red flags

Reduce or reject the thesis if:
- Fusion activity remains low or cannot be independently measured.
- The token can be bypassed for most meaningful customer usage and no compensating demand mechanism exists.
- The Clearing House announcement does not progress to operational deployment or Quant's role proves peripheral.
- Competing systems win institutional deployments through stronger distribution, lower integration costs or better regulatory acceptance.
- Wallet concentration, insider balances or market depth make the apparent scarcity misleading.
- The market price already discounts adoption and token utility that have not materialised.

## 17. The emerging financial system — where Quant could sit

**Research update: 20 November 2026**

### The structural transition

The likely future is not one global blockchain replacing the banking system. It is a layered, interoperable financial architecture in which regulated money and financial assets exist in several forms and on several networks:

1. **Money layer:** commercial-bank deposits, tokenised deposits, regulated payment stablecoins and, potentially, central-bank digital money.
2. **Asset layer:** tokenised government securities, funds, bonds, private-market assets and other financial instruments.
3. **Ledger layer:** public blockchains, permissioned bank ledgers, market-infrastructure networks and proprietary institutional systems.
4. **Connectivity and orchestration layer:** identity, permissions, compliance, messaging, transaction coordination, liquidity routing and cross-ledger settlement.
5. **Legacy access and settlement layer:** existing bank accounts, payment systems, clearing houses, custody, central securities depositories and legal/regulatory frameworks.

The opportunity is created by fragmentation. A tokenised deposit on one bank's permissioned ledger does not automatically become interoperable with a stablecoin on a public chain or a tokenised bond on a separate market network. Institutions need to coordinate assets and obligations without losing control, compliance or the legal status of the underlying money.

This is the infrastructure problem Quant is targeting. Overledger is positioned as connectivity and orchestration; Fusion is positioned as a multi-ledger execution environment. Quant's own mainnet announcement describes Fusion as connecting 74 networks and representing assets from multiple chains in a unified execution environment. That is a company-reported product capability, not yet independently measured production throughput or economic adoption.

### Swift: incumbent adapting, not standing still

It is strategically inaccurate to underwrite QNT on the assumption that Swift is obsolete or will simply disappear. Swift has an enormous installed base, institutional trust, standards influence and bank connectivity. It is actively extending its infrastructure into tokenised finance.

The evidence is now stronger than a proof of concept:
- On 9 July 2026, Swift said its blockchain-based ledger was ready for initial use and that 17 banks across six continents were preparing tokenised-deposit pilots.
- On 19 August 2026, Standard Chartered and HSBC announced the first live cross-border tokenised-deposit transaction on Swift's ledger. The ledger coordinated and matched tokenised deposit obligations; final settlement still used existing systems.
- Swift's own description positions the ledger as an extension of its trusted global platform, not as a wholesale replacement of existing banking infrastructure.

This is both market validation and competitive evidence. Swift is proving that it can adapt, mobilise banks and coordinate tokenised deposits. Its distribution advantage is formidable.

### Quant's potential position is different — and potentially complementary

The Clearing House selection is important because it gives Quant a defined role in a separate US bank-led initiative. The Clearing House said Quant would provide the interoperability, orchestration and transaction-management layer for tokenised-deposit clearing and settlement, with connectivity to RTP and CHIPS. The initiative is expected to become available to participating institutions in H1 2027.

The distinction matters:
- Swift is building a shared ledger and orchestration capability within its global banking network.
- The Clearing House initiative is designed around US bank-to-bank tokenised-deposit clearing and settlement, connected to RTP and CHIPS.
- Quant's role is the technology layer selected by The Clearing House for that initiative.
- Fusion's broader proposition is cross-ledger execution across public and permissioned networks.

These are overlapping markets, but not necessarily identical products or mutually exclusive systems. The future could involve competition, integration, coexistence or consolidation. A bank may use Swift for global messaging and network access, a domestic clearing infrastructure for local settlement, and a specialist interoperability/execution layer to connect tokenised assets and ledgers. The exact architecture will be determined by institutions, regulators, market infrastructures and technical performance.

**Investment interpretation:** Quant does not need to destroy Swift to win. It needs to become a valuable and difficult-to-replace infrastructure provider within a multi-network financial system. However, Swift's own ledger means Quant must demonstrate a differentiated use case, not merely claim that interoperability is needed.

### Why the Clearing House selection is a high-value proof point

The Clearing House says its payment networks clear and settle more than $2 trillion each day. Its selection of Quant is therefore more significant than an ordinary technology partnership announcement. It puts Quant in the architecture of a serious bank-led programme connected to established payment rails.

The evidence boundary remains essential:
- Confirmed: The Clearing House publicly announced Quant's selection and described Quant's intended technical role.
- Confirmed: The initiative is designed for tokenised-deposit clearing and settlement and integration with RTP and CHIPS.
- Confirmed: availability to participating institutions is expected in H1 2027.
- Not yet confirmed: production launch, number of live participating banks, transaction volume, recurring Quant revenue, contract value, or QNT purchases/staking arising from the deployment.

The next milestone is not another announcement. It is proof that the network launches, banks participate, real transactions settle and Quant's role generates durable commercial value.

### Strategic scenarios

| Scenario | What happens | Implication for Quant |
|---|---|---|
| Multi-network coexistence | Swift, domestic payment networks, bank ledgers and public chains remain; interoperability becomes more important | Attractive opportunity if Quant wins cross-system workflows |
| Incumbent-led consolidation | Swift and major market infrastructures extend their own ledgers and integration stacks | Quant must prove unique capability or become a supplier/partner to incumbents |
| Specialist infrastructure wins | Banks use several specialist providers for tokenisation, compliance, orchestration and settlement | Quant can win a valuable layer, but must defend margins and integrations |
| Fragmented adoption | Pilots proliferate but production remains slow, local and low-volume | Company opportunity and QNT demand may disappoint despite a strong narrative |

### What would establish a durable Quant moat?

We should look for evidence of:
- Production integrations that are costly or operationally risky to replace.
- Repeat business across multiple institutions and jurisdictions.
- A growing catalogue of live workflows, not just network connections.
- Demonstrable security, resilience, compliance and operational performance.
- Transaction volume and value settled across independent ledgers.
- Contracted recurring revenue and customer retention.
- QNT-denominated fees, staking requirements or other verifiable token demand.

Patents, standards participation, network count and senior institutional relationships are supporting evidence. They are not substitutes for customer dependence, production economics or token value capture.

### Revised investment judgement

The macro thesis is compelling: financial assets and money are becoming programmable, while the underlying infrastructure remains fragmented. That creates a need for connectivity and orchestration across regulated and open networks.

Quant is positioned directly in that problem and has a meaningful institutional proof point in The Clearing House selection. Fusion's multi-ledger design could broaden the opportunity beyond a single payments corridor or blockchain ecosystem.

But the investment thesis must not rely on the claim that Swift is old and therefore doomed. Swift is actively modernising and has already demonstrated a live tokenised-deposit transaction with major banks. Quant's opportunity is to be a differentiated connective layer in a system where incumbents and specialists may coexist.

**Committee stance:** positive on the structural market and increasingly constructive on Quant's strategic positioning. Maintain a separate, evidence-led hurdle for QNT: prove that the company's institutional success translates into persistent token demand, meaningful staking/lock-up or fee consumption. Do not confuse a large addressable market with automatic token appreciation.

## 18. Phase IV — preliminary on-chain supply and holder-concentration audit

**Research update:** 20 November 2026  
**Evidence class:** public Ethereum explorer data plus independent third-party event-index analysis. This is a preliminary audit, not a complete forensic wallet attribution.

### A. Critical distinction: reported supply, contract balance and circulating float

A fresh QNT supply review identifies a material distinction that should be explicit in every future model:

- The QNT contract's `totalSupply()` returns **45,467,000**, but the verified contract code hardcodes this value rather than updating it to reflect tokens actually minted. It is not a reliable measure of issued or circulating QNT.
- The QNT token contract address itself holds approximately **9,550,897 QNT** in its own balance (20 November 2026 snapshot). An independent review of the verified source code concludes that no function in the contract can transfer this balance out. This is consistent with the 2018 unsold-token burn, although the current contract balance is about 5,000 QNT higher than the historical burn figure; the explanation for the difference has not been independently established.
- The same independent review counted approximately **24,431,259 QNT** across 608 mint events, all on 25 June 2018, with no later mint events found in its indexed history. Subtracting the contract-held balance leaves approximately **14,880,362 QNT**, which is about **267,869 QNT higher** than the 14,612,493 reported total supply used in the Blockworks filing.
- Blockworks' filing reports **14,612,493 total** and **14,544,176 circulating**. CoinGecko's 20 November 2026 snapshot reports approximately **14,556,178 circulating**.

**Committee treatment:** retain 14,612,493 as the reported-supply denominator for comparability with the filing, and 14,544,176 as the dated filing's circulating denominator, but flag the approximately 267.9k difference between the third-party mint-event reconstruction and reported total as unresolved. Do not present the on-chain event reconstruction as an issuer-confirmed supply figure. The 9.55m contract balance should be treated as effectively inaccessible on the available code reading, not as freely tradable float. A specialist smart-contract audit would be required before calling the balance irreversibly burned as a formal legal/technical conclusion.

### C. Exchange-labelled holdings — a floor, not a float estimate

The same dashboard identifies **1,426,636 QNT** across 11 verified exchange-labelled addresses within its top-50 list, equal to approximately **9.76%** of reported supply. Examples include Uphold, Binance, Crypto.com, Kraken, eToro, CoinSpot, Gate and other exchange-labelled addresses.

This is only a **labelled-address lower bound**. It is not total exchange custody, not net exchange reserves, and not an estimate of tokens immediately available for sale. Some exchange wallets may hold customer assets; many exchange addresses may be unlabelled; exchange hot/cold wallet movements can be internal transfers. No net exchange-flow or effective-float conclusion is justified from this snapshot alone.

### D. What this preliminary audit does and does not establish

**Established to a reasonable preliminary standard:**
- The 45.467m contract return is a hardcoded constant and should not be used as circulating supply.
- Approximately 9.55m QNT sits at the token contract address; the independent source-code review finds no apparent transfer path for that balance.
- The top non-contract addresses are materially concentrated: the top ten account for roughly 24.5% of the reported supply denominator in the third-party tracker.
- Verified exchange-labelled addresses in the top 50 account for at least approximately 9.76% of reported supply.

**Not established:**
- Beneficial ownership of the large unlabelled wallets.
- Whether the two 600k wallets are controlled by the same party.
- The full set of exchange, custodian, bridge, treasury, team or market-maker addresses.
- Actual net exchange reserves or freely sellable float.
- A complete reconciliation between mint-event balances, contract-held tokens and the issuer/filing supply figure.
- Current spot order-book depth, slippage or market impact.
